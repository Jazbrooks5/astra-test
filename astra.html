Astra: a game AI with a temperament

Astra learns to play while it plays, by trial and error. No LLM, no GPU, no pre-written decision tree. A simulated hormonal system (dopamine, cortisol, adrenaline) sets its rhythm: how much it explores, how long it sticks with a move, how much risk it takes.

Change the hormones' physiology, and the same AI fights with a different character.

Watch it fight

P1 (top-left health bar) is Astra, in FightingICE, against MctsAi23i, a Monte-Carlo tree search opponent.

What we measured

Two temperaments, same brain: bold (dopamine very reactive, stress cleared fast) and nervous (stress very reactive and slow to clear). Three independent lives each, 20 rounds per life, analysis written before the runs.

82%of 120 rounds: a classifier names the temperament from play style alone (chance: 50%)
22% vs 5%of decisions repeat the previous move: the bold one sticks with what works
2.4×more airborne moves (jumps, aerial attacks) for the nervous one, with a more varied move set
58 / 60rounds won by each temperament against MctsAi23i

Astra also learns within a single life, and it beats a random player by about 110 to 125 health points per round against the same opponent.

What we do not claim

  • The hormones do not make Astra stronger. A first run suggested they did; it did not replicate on three new lives.
  • Whether human players actually see the temperament difference is still open: that is what the 90-second test measures.

Why it might matter for your game

  • NPCs and opponents with a stable personality that emerges from play, instead of hand-tuned scripts.
  • Light: learns online, runs on an ordinary CPU, no network calls.
  • The same mechanism is being tried in other genres (racing, sandbox).
